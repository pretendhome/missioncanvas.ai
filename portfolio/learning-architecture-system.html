Every product ships an SDK for machines. Nobody ships one for people.

An SDK for humans is a map of the stable problems a product solves, a knowledge library with grounded answers, and a delivery system that puts the right answer in front of the right person at the right moment. It's measured by changed behavior, not by content consumed. This page is the system I design and build to do that, at Mission Canvas and for the teams we enable.

01 · The thesis

One graph, two runtimes. Agents and people learn from the same map.

At Mission Canvas, every request is classified against a map of stable problems before an AI acts. The same map is the backbone of the curriculum. An agent meets a node as context; a person meets it as a module. The curriculum isn't bolted onto the knowledge base: it is the knowledge base, seen through a learning lens.

"The SDK for LLMs routes agents to the right context. The SDK for Humans routes people to the right competency. One graph, one planner, two runtimes."From the integration spec

02 · The system

Six layers, and a knowledge-velocity loop that keeps them current.

A single lab, however good, isn't enablement. Enablement is a system that serves the same knowledge in different modes, at different depths, for different roles, and stays current as the product ships every week. People pull ("teach me this", "fix this now"); the product pushes (a release, a stale page).

"Every feedback path terminates in an action. No feedback is just recorded. All feedback acts."From the knowledge-velocity design

06 · The coaching engine Built · in Palette

An engine and a domain pack, so the same coach can teach anything.

The coach never asks "what do you want to work on?"; it proposes the next useful move. Sessions run 20 to 40 minutes. Behind them is a learner lens with four parts, identity, assessment, goals and state, that the learner owns and can carry anywhere. The assessment shapes the coaching; it's never shown back as a score. The engine went through eight design iterations, and was first shaped on adaptive learning for people with learning differences.

07 · A lab, end to end

"Connect your first MCP server": 90 minutes, a check at the end of every part.

One macro-depth artifact from the matrix, built for technical field teams. It's the fifth module of a twelve-week ramp in four phases: foundations, extend, deploy, expert. Each section ends with a check, for example: explain the value in two sentences without saying "protocol".

08 · The content engine Built

From a signal to a published lesson, in one loop.

Enablement goes stale the week a product ships. The content engine turns a signal (a question asked twice, a release, a failed check) into a short "how to do X" lesson with a learning path that pastes into any AI tool. Then it listens for the next signal.

Principles

The curriculum is the knowledge base

Projected through a learning lens, not bolted on. One source, two kinds of enablement, one measurement system.

Behavior, not consumption

Success is ramp time, milestones hit and escalations avoided. Views and completions are not outcomes.

Evidence has a grade

Every claim is marked measured, cited, inferred or "cannot tell". The system says what it doesn't know, and so do its learners.

Adaptive never means hidden

The path changes as the learner changes; the standard doesn't. The learner can always see why the next step was chosen.

Learner records stay local

Progress and assessment live on the learner's own machine by default. Cloud scoring happens only when asked for, and with redaction.

Builder of builders

Design the systems, standards and interfaces that let other people learn and deliver well, without the original expert in the room.
